# EMF 
## A long weekend of geekery in an internet-connected field.

From the [Badgethon](https://bobyn.uk/Badgeathon.html) I won two tickets to attend [EMF](https://www.emfcamp.org/) a biennial festival for all things hacker, maker, artistic, crafty and scientific, a temporary town that posits itself as being for all those with an inquisitive mind. From my experience, I found a place where the culture of creativity and technology goofed off, joked around, let its hair down and flourished. It's important to note that EMF is 100% volunteer ran and self organised, there's no big company looking to make a profit and even orga have to buy their own ticket, and the best way to get a guaranteed ticket is to volunteer, give a talk, run a workshop, or build an installation.

# Build a village

A big part of how EMF self organizes is through villages, a village can be based around themes, shared passions and existing communities, pick a point on the map and declare that you will be pitching your tent there(ish) and anyone interested in your theme will know where to go to meet like minded people. A few of my favourite villages include:
- [Gladstonledburshire on sever](https://www.emfcamp.org/villages/2026/28-glastonledburyshire-on-severn): What do you get when you steal the uk gov branding and start annexing other villages.
- [Scottish consulate](https://www.emfcamp.org/villages/2026/37-scottish-consulate): Scotland's "official" presence.
- [Milliways](https://www.emfcamp.org/villages/2026/35-milliways): Partied the latest and feeding the hungry.

But there are so many more, from biohackers to lockpickers along with just about every hackerspace in the country.

# A festival with proper networking
Any festival you go to these days you can assume the network will be congested beyond use and you won't be able to phone your friends and you'll be perpetually lost and seperated, but that just wouldn't stand for this kind of crowd. EMF spins up it's own infrastructure with a comprehensive network for wifi, GSM, LTE and DECT phones and if that wasn't enough they go to the trouble of setting up a plain old telephone service with public phones dotted around the festival that you can even get your own landline phone wired into.
<img src="images/EMF_phone.jpg" alt="An example of one of the public phones at EMF">
What's networking without a little power though, won't be able to use all that if your phone or laptop dies, so if you bring the cable, EMF will bring the power for just about anything you can reasonably think of (yes even a kettle!) right to your tent and to top it all off you can even get Ethernet! All of this is provided through the wonders of the eponymous Datenklo or by another name a "data toilet" because it turns out if you want reliable cheap waterproof outdoor storage for all that kit, portaloos are a very convenient option.
<img src="images/EMF_datenklo.jpg" alt="A Datenklo in all it's electrical glory">
Even the bar has networking so you can check live drink inventory anywhere in the festival.

## Who you gonna call?

Many festivals have artistic installations, but all pale compared to a what you can get done with a working phone system. At all the public phones at EMF you can find people reaching out and advertising their own weird little corner of the festival, on stickers, posters and scrawled in pen providing any crazed service you could dream of.

## Swap shop
We've all been there, odd bits of wire, old computers, forgotten hardware, whatever it is, there's a home for it as the [swap shop](https://www.emfcamp.org/about/swap-shop), a place where people bring the random junk that’s been sitting in drawers, and go home with some different random junk which just might get turned into something amazing. 

## The Data emporium
The [Data Emporium](where people bring the random junk that’s been sitting in drawers, and go home with some different random junk which just might get turned into something amazing.) is where all the data lives, there's data everywhere in our modern world, but somtimes it can get a bit lonely, so the data emporium was a lovely place to collect it all together in one place and to talk about the joy of counting, noting, and just knowing about data.


## Big toys and games
EMF features a frankly overwhelming number large installations to play with, from giant rubix cubes to a vintage arcade and even fire pong! you'll find an immich album of some of the ones I remembered to document.
A few of my favourites include a scratch style physical interface for strudel (a music programming language), a receipt printer continously printing a [strange poetic story](https://worldenddisk.com/projects/kiss-garden/), a [towers of hannoi](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3waVV7Fjct0) endurance world record and a fluid simulation ran on [flip dots](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aZzOWnEWgww) that sounded like a million butterfly.

## Nullsector
What's a festival without a little nightlife? EMF goes all out with a fully themed club which this year took the form of the polybius biotech spaceport set in the year 4242 featuring a number of shipping containers with their own exhibits

### Night & Day market

One of the best parts of getting a bunch of techy people together is getting to take some tech home with you to play with and at emf there was a formal night market with stands and stalls of people hawking their projects in the middle of nullsector, from little robots, music synths, soldering kit, 
<img src="images/EMF_nightMarket1.jpg" alt="Some cute LED PCB art pieces">

In the day when the ravers are in bed, the space is used to congregate as a space to less formally buy, sell and swap fun bits of tech or art they've made, primarily hexpansions for the Hexagon badge, but also similar fare as you could find in the night market. There plenty to be found, from LoRa and meshtastic gear to NFC tags and I even managed to trade one of my NFC ceramic fortune cookies I made for a glowing rubber duck hexpansion.
<img src="images/EMF_dayMarket1.jpg" alt="Buying cat ear hexpansion at the day market">

You can even just bump into people selling stuff, I came spotted several people sporting LED cat ears which lead me down an adventurous night of raving with some lovely folks from the birkenhack space and ended with my very own pair of [rudelblinken](https://github.com/zebreus/rudelblinken) ears. With UK standard plug sockets of course.
<img src="images/EMF_rudelblinken.jpg" alt="rudelblinken cat ears">
## *The* Badge

We seem to have come full circle, I earned my place at EMF with a badge, and at EMF I was presented with a *badge* - presenting the Hexagon.
<img src="images/EMF_hexagon.jpg" alt="The EMF hexagon badge">
The concept of a conference badge has grown from a humble beginning - spawned at DEFCON - to a whole host of crazy devices that only nominally resemble a badge and can often look more like full blown computers in their own rights (could the outpace the apollo computers?) and are generally a wonderful opportunity to create, play around, exchange ideas and showoff.
